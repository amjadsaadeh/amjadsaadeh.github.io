I am in 3d printing for a while and learned to tinker around with CAD software.
It's fun, but designing everything from scratch is pretty tedious, so I decided to give claude a try to create something for me.

# The part
The part itself is a hitch to connect a toy car with a trailer.
A knob broke last year and the part itself gathered dust while resting on my desk.
Simple enough to replicate it, but still a bunch of work, to defer it, especially since I am lacking routine in using CAD.

# The plan
My plan was to upload some images in the style of a character sheet and give significant measurements of some parts.
In my mind this way Claude should see all necessary angles and also be able to derive the correct sizes from the image.
As model I used Fable 5.2 since I got some unused quota in my subscription.

# The process
Overall the process was pretty smooth.
It didn't prefectly work out on the first promp, some features of the part hadn't been recognized correctly.
But a few messages later I got a pretty close match.
A very nice feature was the preview image, so I didn't need to download the STL file on every step.

![Final preview of the 3d rendered part](/blog-images/004/final-preview.png)

I published the [complete conversation](https://claude.ai/share/465f709d-5de0-49dd-bb22-336dd0c7511b) if you are interested in the details.
I apologize for bad writing.
I did it on my smartphone, german autocorrect hit in from time to time and I don't care so much about correct writing with Claude, since it catches my intention most of the time anyway.

# The result

After printing the result looks pretty good:

![final result comparison](/blog-images/004/final-result-1.jpg)

And it also fits, even though I need to disassemble the trailer a bit more then expected:

![final result assembled](/blog-images/004/final-result-2.jpg)

The experience was pretty smooth and saved me a lot of time, at least I assume that I would've need more time for designing by hand.
I was also pretty impressed how well my instructions had been followed, despite my bad writing.
