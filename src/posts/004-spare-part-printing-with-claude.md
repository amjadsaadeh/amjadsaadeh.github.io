I have been into 3D printing for a while and have learned to tinker with CAD software.
It's fun, but designing everything from scratch is pretty tedious, so I decided to give Claude a try.

# The part
The part is a hitch that connects a toy car to a trailer.
A knob broke off last year, and the part has been gathering dust on my desk ever since.
It's simple enough to replicate, but still a fair amount of work, especially since I lack routine in CAD. That's why I kept putting it off.

# The plan
My plan was to upload some images in the style of a character sheet and provide the key measurements of the part.

![The views onto the part](/blog-images/004/views.png)

In my mind, this way Claude would see all the necessary angles and be able to derive the correct sizes from the images.
I used Fable 5.2 because I had some unused quota left in my subscription.

# The process
Overall, the process was pretty smooth.
It didn't work perfectly on the first prompt, as some features of the part weren't recognized correctly.
But a few messages later, I had a pretty close match.
A very nice feature was the preview image, which meant I didn't need to download the STL file at every step.

![Final preview of the 3D-rendered part](/blog-images/004/final-preview.png)

I published the [complete conversation](https://claude.ai/share/465f709d-5de0-49dd-bb22-336dd0c7511b) in case you are interested in the details.
I apologize for the bad writing.
I wrote it on my smartphone, and German autocorrect kicked in from time to time.
I also don't care much about correct writing when talking to Claude, since it catches my intention most of the time anyway.

# The result

After printing, the result looks pretty good:

![Final result comparison](/blog-images/004/final-result-1.jpg)

It also fits, even though I need to disassemble the trailer a bit more than expected:

![Final result assembled](/blog-images/004/final-result-2.jpg)

The experience was pretty smooth and saved me a lot of time, at least I assume that designing it by hand would have taken longer.
I was also impressed by how well my instructions were followed, despite my bad writing.
